## SubQ as the judge and query translator (E9-1)

SubQ Snap (`subq-snap-preview`) against the LLM judge (Haiku, `JUDGE_THRESHOLD` 0.8) and the local rankers on the evaluation set: 20 queries, labels graded by Claude and not yet reviewed (#79). Every LLM role ran on Haiku through the Claude Code subscription. Full `eval:run` report: `reports/eval-20261015T185919Z.md`.

### The judge

`npm run eval:run` with `SUBQ_API_KEY`. The `tags+jev` rows equal `tags+llm`: there is no JEV key, so the LLM answered for JEV. Each judge saw the same 30 tag candidates per query. The sweep keeps at most `RERANK_TOP_K` (8) per query.

| Judge         | Recommended threshold | Central P | Central R | Central F1 | All P | All R | All F1 |
| ------------- | --------------------- | --------- | --------- | ---------- | ----- | ----- | ------ |
| `llm` (Haiku) | 0.8                   | 0.416     | 0.335     | **0.371**  | 0.707 | 0.222 | 0.338  |
| `subq`        | 0.6                   | 0.304     | 0.360     | **0.330**  | 0.613 | 0.243 | 0.348  |

- **SubQ's threshold sweep.** It is flat from 0.1 to 0.8: central F1 runs from 0.312 to 0.330, with a peak at 0.6. At 0.9 recall falls to 0.184.
- **Score distribution.** SubQ's scores are more decisive than the LLM's. 24 % fall below 0.1, against 5 % for the LLM.
- **Queries with nothing approved.** SubQ approved at least one candidate for every query at 0.5. The LLM approved none for one query. JEV's reported weakness, rejecting every candidate, does not show for SubQ here.
- **By document type, every graded chunk.** SubQ keeps more of what is relevant:
  - **Statutes:** F1 0.524 against 0.449 (recall 0.487 against 0.384).
  - **Rulings:** F1 0.310 against 0.238.
- **By document type, central chunks.** The LLM is ahead on statutes (0.406 against 0.364) and on rulings (0.223 against 0.206).
- **Ranking.** In the comparison above, SubQ ranks better than every other reranker:
  - **Recall@8 after rerank, central:** SubQ 0.322, LLM 0.276, minilm 0.203, bge 0.178.
  - **P@5, every graded chunk:** SubQ 0.630, LLM 0.530.
- **Time.** The rerank of 30 candidates takes 1.3 s per query with SubQ against 26.4 s with the LLM: 20 times faster. minilm takes 3.1 s and bge 26.1 s.

### The main flow with SubQ

`bench:compare -- --runs 1` with `RERANKER=subq`; the reference RAG still judges with the LLM. The main flow has its sources in **1.5 s** after translation, against 26–28 s with the LLM judge in E7-8 to E7-10. Its total time (48 s) is now mostly the draft (39 s). Precision of the chunks sent is 0.590 and recall 0.241, against 0.641 and 0.223 with the LLM judge on the same labels (E7-10). Source accuracy is 0.976, and every query got a draft.

### The default rules

- **`RERANKER`: stays `llm`.** The rule needs SubQ's central F1 to be at least the LLM's, at half its rerank time or less. The time holds (1.3 s against 26.4 s). The F1 does not (0.330 against 0.371).
- **`QUERY_TRANSLATOR_IMPL`: stays `llm`.** The rule needs SubQ's R@cand to be at least the LLM's, at half its translation time or less. The time holds (1.0 s against 7.1 s). The recall does not (central 0.332 against 0.399).
- **SubQ's `JUDGE_THRESHOLD` default: 0.6**, the sweep's recommendation. `QUERY_TRANSLATOR_THRESHOLD` stays 0.5.
- **Caveat.** One labeller, 20 queries, and the gap in central F1 (0.04) is about the size of the run-to-run noise seen in E7-7. SubQ wins on ranking, on every graded chunk and on time, so the owner may prefer it for speed; that is the owner's decision, not the rule's.
